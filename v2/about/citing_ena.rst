==========
Citing ENA
==========

There are two different things you might cite: ENA itself as a resource, or a specific dataset held in ENA.

Citing the archive
==================

If you use ENA in your work, please cite our latest paper in the Nucleic Acids Research database issue. The current one is:

.. epigraph::

    "Rahman N, Ahamed A, Devaraj R, et al. The European Nucleotide Archive in 2026. Nucleic Acids Res. 2026.. `<https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkag1162>`_"

Citing data in ENA
==================

Cite the accession of the data you deposited, with a link to its record. In most cases this is the top-level project accession, for example:

.. epigraph::
    
    "The data for this study have been deposited in the European Nucleotide Archive (ENA) 
    at EMBL-EBI under accession number PRJEBxxxx (``https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/browser/view/PRJEBxxxx``)."

If one project accession would be ambiguous, for example when several papers each use a different part of the same project, cite a more specific accession instead:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 83 17
   :width: 100%

   * - What you are citing
     - Example accession
   * - An assembly
     - GCA_123456789.1
   * - A sample, in the context of its associated data
     - SAMEA123456
   * - An assembled or annotated sequence, such as a contig, scaffold or chromosome from an assembly submission
     - A12345.1
